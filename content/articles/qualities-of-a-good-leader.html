The most important qualities of a good leader are integrity, clear communication, the ability to listen, accountability, empathy, courage, and a willingness to serve. A good leader earns trust, gives people direction, and helps others grow. None of these traits is something you are simply born with. Each one can be practised, usually by leading something small and learning from it.

This guide breaks down twelve qualities, explains why each matters, and gives a simple way to build it, whether you lead a barangay youth council, a school club, a small business or a volunteer project.

Why the qualities of a good leader matter

A title does not make a leader. People follow someone because they trust them, understand where they are going, and feel respected along the way. In Filipino culture, we often say a leader should have malasakit, a genuine concern for others that shows in action. Most of the qualities below are ways of putting that care into practice.

1. Integrity

What it means: Doing what you say, telling the truth, and being the same person in public and in private.

Why it matters: Without trust, nothing else works. People will not follow a leader they doubt.

How to build it: Make fewer promises and keep all of them. Be open about money, especially in organizations that handle funds from members or sponsors.

2. Clear communication

What it means: Explaining goals, roles and decisions so everyone understands them.

Why it matters: Confusion wastes time and causes conflict.

How to build it: Practise summarizing a plan in three sentences. Speak up at meetings and events; our public speaking tips can help.

3. Active listening

What it means: Listening to understand, not just to reply.

Why it matters: Your team often sees problems before you do. Listening also makes people feel valued.

How to build it: In your next conversation, repeat back what you heard before giving your opinion. Ask quieter members for their ideas directly.

4. Accountability

What it means: Owning your results, including mistakes.

Why it matters: When leaders blame others, teams stop taking risks. When leaders own mistakes, teams learn.

How to build it: After every project, run a short review: what worked, what did not, and what you personally will do differently.

5. Empathy

What it means: Understanding how others feel and what they are going through.

Why it matters: Volunteers and team members juggle work, school and family. An empathetic leader plans around real lives.

How to build it: Check in with people as people, not only about tasks. Ask, "How are you, really?"

6. Courage

What it means: Making hard decisions, speaking up when something is wrong, and trying new things.

Why it matters: Change in any community starts with someone willing to go first.

How to build it: Take on one task this month that scares you a little, such as pitching to a sponsor or leading a meeting.

7. Vision

What it means: Seeing a better future and helping others see it too.

Why it matters: A clear goal keeps a team moving when the work gets tiring.

How to build it: Write one sentence describing what success looks like a year from now. Share it and ask for feedback.

8. Decisiveness

What it means: Making a timely decision with the information you have.

Why it matters: Waiting too long can be worse than making an imperfect choice.

How to build it: Set a deadline for each decision. Gather input, decide, then explain your reasons.

9. Humility

What it means: Knowing you do not have all the answers and giving others credit.

Why it matters: Humble leaders keep learning and attract good people.

How to build it: Thank people publicly and by name. Ask for help when you need it.

10. Resilience

What it means: Recovering from setbacks and keeping going.

Why it matters: Events get rained out, sponsors pull out and plans change. In Capiz, typhoon season alone can reshape a project calendar.

How to build it: Always have a Plan B. After a setback, focus on the next step rather than on blame.

11. Ability to develop others

What it means: Helping people grow into leaders themselves.

Why it matters: A group that depends on one person falls apart when that person leaves.

How to build it: Delegate real responsibility, not just errands. Pair new members with experienced ones.

Chapter example: In JCI Roxas Halaran, members who finish the GABAY Trainer's Certification and Mentoring Program go on to train others. Secretary John Moses Aposaga completed GABAY Year 5 in 2026, and in June 2026 he and VP for Internal Raymark Bigcas, a 2025 GABAY graduate, led training sessions for the YLEA 2026 youth leadership program.

12. A heart for service

What it means: Leading to benefit others, not to gain status.

Why it matters: People can tell the difference between a leader who serves and one who wants applause.

How to build it: Volunteer for the unglamorous jobs: setting up chairs, cleaning up, staying until the end. This is the core of servant leadership, one of the types of leadership that works especially well in volunteer groups.

Chapter example: Our Hot Meals on Wheels program brings hot meals to residents, runners and volunteers. Quick self-check: how do you rate?

Score yourself from 1 (rarely) to 5 (always) on each statement. Then pick one low score to work on this month.

  • I keep the promises I make to my team.
  • People understand what I am asking them to do.
  • I listen fully before I respond.
  • I admit my mistakes openly.
  • I know what is going on in my teammates' lives.
  • I speak up when something is wrong.
  • I can explain where our group is going.
  • I make decisions on time.
  • I give credit to others.
  • I bounce back quickly from setbacks.
  • I am training someone to take my place.
  • I do the work I ask others to do.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important quality of a good leader?

Many people would say integrity, because trust is the foundation for everything else. Without it, even a skilled communicator will struggle to lead.

Can leadership qualities be learned?

Yes. Traits like listening, decisiveness and resilience grow with practice, feedback and experience. Leading small projects is one of the best ways to develop them.

What are the qualities of a good student leader?

The same qualities apply: integrity, communication, listening, accountability and service. Student leaders also need good time management to balance leadership with their studies.

How is a leader different from a boss?

A boss relies on position to get things done. A leader earns cooperation through trust, example and care for the people they lead.
